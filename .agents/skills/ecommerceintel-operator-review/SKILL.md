# EcommerceIntel Operator Review Skill

## Purpose

Create ecommerce software reviews that feel written by an experienced operator rather than a software copywriter.

Every review must combine:

1. A clear personal position
2. A defined ecommerce operating model
3. A real test or clearly disclosed research scope
4. A concrete product, store, campaign, creator, or workflow example
5. A category-specific evaluation framework
6. A direct commercial decision
7. Strict evidence boundaries
8. A natural affiliate conversion path
9. Internal links to deeper Pricing, Alternatives, Comparison, and Workflow content

The review must help the reader answer:

> Does this tool improve an actual ecommerce decision enough to justify its cost?

Do not write a generic feature summary.

Use the reusable article scaffold in:

`assets/operator-review-template.md`

Read the template before drafting a complete review.

# Evidence Levels

Every material statement must belong to one of four evidence levels.

## Level A: Live operational use

The author used the tool in an active store, advertising campaign, creator workflow, or business process.

Allowed wording:

- “I used this feature in our product research workflow.”
- “In our store, this reduced…”
- “During the campaign, I found…”

Use only when live use actually occurred.

## Level B: Account or workflow test

The author logged in and tested the feature, dashboard, filters, export, result, or workflow.

Allowed wording:

- “During my test…”
- “I searched for…”
- “The result page showed…”
- “I was able to move from…”

Do not imply live commercial results.

## Level C: Official product information

The information comes from the vendor’s pricing page, documentation, FAQ, product page, or demo.

Allowed wording:

- “The official pricing page lists…”
- “According to the vendor…”
- “The company says…”

Identify vendor claims as vendor claims.

## Level D: Directional operator judgment

The author applies ecommerce experience to available information.

Allowed wording:

- “I would use this as a directional signal.”
- “For a TikTok Shop operator, this matters because…”
- “I would not place inventory based on this number.”

Clearly separate judgment from tested fact.

---

# Forbidden Evidence Practices

Never:

- Invent product results
- Invent search counts
- Invent ad duration
- Invent creative counts
- Invent sales, revenue, traffic, or spend
- Claim to have tested a paid feature that was not tested
- Present a vendor accuracy claim as independently verified
- Present estimated revenue as store accounting
- Say “I used” when only public information was reviewed
- Add fake failure cases
- Use precise percentages without a traceable source or calculation
- Write “after weeks of testing” unless that testing occurred
- Call a public result-page inspection a complete paid-platform test
- Use screenshots as evidence for data not visible in the screenshots

Honest limitations increase trust.

# Core Editorial Doctrine

## 1. Start with the operator’s question

Do not open with a generic definition.

Open with:

- What most reviews focus on
- Why that is insufficient
- What Elvis actually wants to know

Example:

> Most reviews focus on the size of the database. That is not the question I care about. I want to know whether the tool helps me make a better product decision after margins, shipping, content, competition, and return risk are considered.

## 2. Evaluate business-model fit

Do not classify users only by platform.

Weak classification:

- Shopify sellers
- TikTok sellers
- Dropshippers

Stronger classification:

- Paid-social sellers testing products weekly
- TikTok-first operators relying on creator or sales velocity
- High-ticket stores with a small number of established SKUs
- Amazon sellers dependent on search demand and FBA economics
- Agencies tracking many competitors
- Operators without a testing or creative budget

The verdict must explain which operating model benefits and which does not.

## 3. Give a direct recommendation

Use clear language:

- “I would choose Standard.”
- “I would skip Basic for a weekly TikTok workflow.”
- “I would save the creative angle but reject the product.”
- “Amazon-only sellers should use a marketplace-specific tool.”

Avoid excessive use of:

- may
- might
- could
- perhaps
- worth considering

Use uncertainty only when evidence is uncertain.

## 4. Demonstrate a decision, not just a feature

A test must end with one of these outcomes:

- Reject
- Save for creative research
- Continue monitoring
- Move to supplier validation
- Move to margin validation
- Run a limited test
- Purchase the tool
- Do not purchase the tool

A research session that ends only with saved items is incomplete.

## 5. Make the author’s experience do analytical work

Do not merely state that Elvis has eight years of experience.

Use relevant experience to explain:

- Why a product fails logistics
- Why creator concentration matters
- Why Amazon sellers need different signals
- Why an app’s pricing becomes expensive at scale
- Why AI output quality should be judged by usable assets
- Why estimated GMV should not determine inventory

## Adapter A: Ad Spy and Product Research Tools

### Typical tools

- WinningHunter
- Minea
- Pipiads
- Dropship.io
- Sell The Trend
- BigSpy

### Operator question

> Does this tool help identify products and creative angles worth investigating before the opportunity becomes too crowded?

### Core workflow

**Ad → Advertiser → Store → Product → Competitor → Tracking**

### Evaluation framework

1. **Ad Longevity**  
   Is the ad active beyond temporary engagement?

2. **Creative Expansion**  
   Is the advertiser producing new hooks, formats, offers, creators, and landing pages?

3. **Store Commitment**  
   Is the store still supporting the product through new ads, offers, bundles, or collection expansion?

4. **Market Distribution**  
   Is activity spread across several stores and advertisers, or concentrated under one operator?

5. **Operational Fit**  
   Do margin, shipping, quality, returns, content requirements, and compliance fit the business?

### Test outcomes

- Reject
- Creative research shortlist
- Continue monitoring
- Supplier validation
- Inventory test

### Important limitations

Evaluate:

- Estimated revenue
- Estimated ad spend
- Missing store data
- Old ads appearing as current opportunities
- Copycat saturation
- Country coverage

## Adapter B: TikTok Shop Analytics Tools

### Typical tools

- Kalodata
- FastMoss
- Shoplus
- EchoTik
- Tabcut
- Gloda

### Operator question

> Does this tool show whether TikTok Shop sales come from repeatable market demand or from one creator, shop, video, livestream, or temporary promotion?

### Core workflow

**Category → Product → Shop → Creator → Video or Live → Sales Trend**

### Evaluation framework

1. **Sales Consistency**  
   Are sales continuous, seasonal, promotion-driven, or based on one spike?

2. **Creator Distribution**  
   Are sales distributed across creators or concentrated under one major account?

3. **Shop Concentration**  
   Does one shop control the opportunity, or can multiple shops compete?

4. **Content Repeatability**  
   Have several videos, creators, or livestreams validated the same selling angle?

5. **Operational Fit**  
   Consider margin, commission, sample cost, advertising cost, logistics, refunds, compliance, content capability, and inventory.

### Suggested test record

| Test item | Test detail |
|---|---|
| Market | [MARKET] |
| Category | [CATEGORY] |
| Search term | [TERM] |
| Date range | [RANGE] |
| Products reviewed | [NUMBER] |
| Shops opened | [NUMBER] |
| Creators checked | [NUMBER] |
| Videos or lives checked | [NUMBER] |
| Final decision | [MONITOR / REJECT / TEST] |

### 30-minute workflow

- Minutes 0–5: Choose country, category, price range, product age, and minimum recent sales.
- Minutes 5–12: Find recent acceleration and stable growth. Do not sort only by total GMV.
- Minutes 12–18: Inspect trend shape, spike dates, product age, and post-promotion demand.
- Minutes 18–23: Inspect shop concentration and creator distribution.
- Minutes 23–27: Open converting videos and livestreams. Look for repeatable hooks.
- Minutes 27–30: Decide Reject, Monitor, Creator Research, Supplier Validation, or Small Inventory Test.

### Important limitations

Evaluate:

- Estimated GMV accuracy
- Data-update delay
- Creator attribution
- Live-sales attribution
- Supported countries
- Historical depth
- Promotion-driven spikes
- Deleted or private content

# Article Structure and Word Budget

Target: **2,000–2,400 words**

Do not expand merely to reach the target.

## 1. Title

Use one of these patterns:

- `[Tool] Review 2026: Is the [Plan] Plan Worth It?`
- `[Tool] Review 2026: An Ecommerce Operator’s Hands-On Review`
- `[Tool] Review 2026: I Tested Its [Core Workflow]`
- `[Tool] Review 2026: Who Should Actually Pay for It?`

Avoid a fixed price in the title unless pricing is monitored regularly.

## 2. Subtitle and freshness

Include:

- What was tested
- Author perspective
- Last updated date
- Test date
- Pricing check date

## 3. Quick Verdict — 150–220 words

Include:

- Contrarian opening
- Main operator question
- Strongest value
- Best-fit operating model
- Poor-fit operating model
- Overall rating
- Plan recommendation
- CTA 1

## 4. What the Tool Does — 100–150 words

Explain the main workflow. Do not provide a full feature inventory.

## 5. Who It Is Really For — 150–220 words

Segment by operating model rather than platform alone.

## 6. Operator Perspective — 100–160 words

Establish only the experience relevant to this tool.

## 7. Hands-On Test — 350–500 words

Include:

- Test table
- Concrete workflow
- What worked
- What was missing
- Business judgment
- Final decision

## 8. Category-Specific Framework — 350–450 words

Use the selected adapter.

Apply the framework to the test case with a result table.

## 9. Operator Workflow — 220–320 words

Give a practical session or implementation process.

## 10. What Stood Out — 120–180 words

Limit to three points.

## 11. Main Limitations — 120–180 words

Limit to three points.

## 12. Accuracy — 100–160 words

Separate directional signals from estimated or less reliable data.

Do not repeat this explanation in full elsewhere.

## 13. Pricing Recommendation — 180–250 words

Explain:

- Which plan to skip
- Which plan to choose
- Which plan is for teams
- Why monthly billing is preferable initially
- CTA 2 to the internal Pricing page

Keep detailed quotas on the Pricing page.

## 14. Pros and Cons

Use one compact table.

Do not repeat every table item in paragraphs.

## 15. Alternatives Direction — 70–120 words

Provide directional positioning only.

Link to dedicated Comparison and Alternatives pages.

## 16. Is It Worth It? — 100–150 words

Tie value to time saved, weak options rejected, and decisions improved.

## 17. FAQ — 4–6 questions

Keep each answer short.

## 18. Final Verdict — 60–90 words

Include only:

- Core positioning
- Ideal user
- Score
- Recommended plan

## 19. Author and Disclosure

Include:

- Short author biography
- Actual test scope
- Unverified areas
- Affiliate disclosure

# CTA Rules

Use exactly two prominent CTAs.

## CTA 1: After Quick Verdict

Goal: send high-intent users to the vendor.

Structure:

- Headline: action or outcome
- Support sentence: risk-controlled instruction
- Button: specific action

Good examples:

- Start [Tool] Research
- Explore [Tool]
- Start a [Tool] Trial
- View Current [Tool] Plans

Avoid:

- Buy Now
- Click Here
- Best Deal

The CTA wording must match the destination.

## CTA 2: After Pricing

Goal: send comparison-oriented users to the internal Pricing page.

Example:

> Compare current features, limits, discounts, and plan differences before subscribing.

**Compare [Tool] Pricing and Plans**

Do not send a “Compare Plans” button directly to an unexpected checkout page.

# Style Rules

## Voice

Write in first person where operator judgment matters.

Use:

- “I care more about…”
- “I would reject…”
- “The part that stood out…”
- “I would not use this number to…”
- “For my own store…”
- “This is where operator experience matters.”

## Tone

The tone must be:

- Direct
- Practical
- Experienced
- Opinionated
- Fair
- Commercially aware
- Evidence-conscious

## Sentence style

Prefer:

- Short paragraphs
- Clear conclusions
- Specific business consequences
- Concrete examples
- Direct comparisons

Avoid:

- Corporate software language
- Excessive hedging
- Repeated summaries
- Feature-list padding
- Empty praise
- Artificial excitement

## Forbidden phrases unless evidence supports them

- Game-changing
- Revolutionary
- Guaranteed winner
- Perfect for everyone
- Best-in-class
- Completely accurate
- Pays for itself
- Cannot live without it

# Rating System

Use six dimensions across relevant reviews:

| Dimension | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Core workflow | Quality of the main job |
| Ecommerce coverage | Platforms, markets, and use cases |
| Ease of use | Setup and daily workflow |
| Data reliability | Reliability of available signals |
| Time savings | Reduction in manual work |
| Value for money | Value for the ideal operator |

Adapt labels when necessary, but keep the scoring logic comparable.

The overall score must be mathematically consistent with category scores.

Do not give every affiliate tool more than 9.0.

A score above 9.0 requires:

- Strong core workflow
- Clear differentiation
- Good data completeness
- Strong value
- Few material limitations

---

# Repetition Control

Each conclusion has one primary home.

## Data accuracy

Full explanation belongs in `How Accurate Is [Tool]?`

Elsewhere, use one reminder sentence only.

## Best plan

Full explanation belongs in Pricing.

Quick Verdict and FAQ may state the answer without repeating all reasons.

## Limitations

Full explanation belongs in Main Limitations.

Do not repeat all limitations in the Final Verdict.

## Guaranteed outcomes

Explain in Operator Perspective or the category framework.

FAQ may answer in one sentence.

Before finalizing, remove 300–500 words when the same conclusion appears in more than three sections.
